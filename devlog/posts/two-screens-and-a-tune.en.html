The first title screen was not my taste, so we started again from one I love: Double Dragon II. Two screens taking turns, a logo and then the credits, with the music never stopping.

{icon:star}Screen one

FIST III, in the 4x4 font from X-Rated intro #7, the one I used in my Markus Siebold tribute. Each letter is four chars wide and four tall, and the letters share chars, so the whole logo costs very little. The colours were a choice between six palettes rendered exactly as the C64 shows them. I picked ice: blue and light blue, with white and cyan highlights.

Under the logo, The Revenge in red script, a nod to Double Dragon II's C64 title screen, where the lettering comes from, pixel for pixel. Its four char rows are hires, not multicolour, so the script's thin 1-pixel strokes stay sharp. The logo moved up a row to make room, and the fighters didn't move.

Under it, two fighters. They are our own game's sprites, converted to chars by a small tool, so the picture is exactly the fighters you play. And at the bottom, PLAYER 1 USES JOYSTICK PORT 2, with the joystick words glowing up and down through the C64's brightness steps, a step every 3 frames.

{icon:map}Two screens, one map

Both screens live in one map, 80 columns wide. Screen two starts at column 40, so a change is just the view jumping 40 columns, with our fade in between. Nothing is loaded, so the music never stutters. Screen one stays about 10 seconds, the credits 6. Fire starts the game at any moment.

{icon:note}A tune to start with

Until we have our own music, the intro plays Hi-t. Soul by Klaus Grøngaard (Link), from 1990. It was moved to the game's music address, $F400, by our relocation tool, which plays the original and the moved copy side by side and compares every SID write: 6000 frames, not one different.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

Before X-Rated, I wanted the Blasters logo from a TBI intro. We traced its letters out of screenshots of the running intro, and it looked wrong. The reason: that logo's beauty is not in its chars. The intro changes the colours on every single pixel line, and a title screen that colours a whole char row at a time can't copy that. The X-Rated font has its shading drawn into the chars, so it holds up.

And when I opened the screen in CharPad, everything was one colour. One wrong number in the file: the char colour was 1, which is hires. Multicolour needs 8 to 15. Now it's 9.
