I sat down to play a test level just to hunt for bugs, and came away with five notes. Four of them became fixes that same evening, and one is still being tracked down.

{icon:star}Numbers in the BAR (or rather, the HUD) that you can read

The numbers in the HUD were almost unreadable: the score, the LEVEL and the CONTINUE countdown. They were still the thin, slanted digits of the original Fist II, sitting next to thick letters we had already replaced. Now the digits are thick too, in the same style as the letters.

One more small thing from the same spot: LEVEL 1 sat right under the score, and when the score was 0 it read "LEVEL 0 1". It moved to the middle of the row, next to where GAME OVER goes.

{icon:chip}High kick, no jump

The flying kick kept clashing with the high kick, and it wasn't clear which one came out when. So we took it out of the game for now: up with fire is now a high kick, same as forward and up. Seven moves, and simpler. The enemies still jump, just not the player.

{icon:map}Two enemies, not one plus one

With two enemies on screen, it felt like only one of them was really fighting.
We measured it, and it turned out both do attack, but each on its own: the Fist II AI was written for one-on-one fights. Each enemy stands and waits a long time before a hit, doesn't know the other one exists, and half the time the second one just waited behind the first, on the same side.

We cut the waits in half. Now both of them press harder, and they come at you from both sides more often. Yum. Sounds scarier to fight now, right?

{icon:note}A "small" effect before GAME OVER

I wanted the end to land like a hit, so before GAME OVER the street shakes side to side for a second and settles. At first it also came with red flashes, arcade style. I tried it, it wasn't to my taste, and an hour later the flashes were out. The short Flash stayed.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

The fifth note was a glitch when the player turns around. We captured every frame of the turn and of the back kick, and it's all clean, so it probably only happens in a fight, with other fighters on screen. Still looking. Alongside that, there's a new page for the artist: what a level means for us, the limits of CharPad, and exactly where on screen the "street" the player moves along is. More on that in the next episodes...

{icon:page}What's next

Find the turn glitch, and maybe teach the second enemy to get around you and attack from behind, like in Double Dragon.
