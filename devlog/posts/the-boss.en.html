Level 1 now has an ending: a boss! Like a lot of classic arcade games from the '80s. My graphics for him aren't drawn yet, and that didn't matter. A boss is mostly rules, so we built the rules first.

{icon:map}One line in the level file

Every level's enemies come from its spawn list, written in the level's json file. One new word turns an entry into a boss: "boss": true. In level 1 he waits in the last room,

at column 640 of the map, a few steps before the exit. Until you beat him, the exit stays shut. No "LEVEL COMPLETE" while he's still standing.

{icon:fist}Who he is (who is this "boss" anyway??)

Regular enemies come in pairs. The boss comes alone, with high-level AI, the highest a level can ask for: he defends himself very well and... he hits back too. For now he's white. On the first try he was purple,
and in the C64 palette that purple sat right next to my player's blue. On screen you just couldn't tell who was who.

Then the energy. A fighter's energy is one byte, so the ceiling is $7f. Instead of rewriting the energy code and the HUD (the block that shows the player's score and energy), the boss got three full bars. Empty one and he falls, gets up, and the bar fills again. An old arcade machine trick. A single jump kick or a sweep won't knock him down either. Only a three-hit combo will.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

Those days also had some quieter work. I asked whether the game's code is written in my style, and we compared it with three of my Crack intros. Mostly it is, closest to the Blazon intro. The leftovers from the 1986 code were cleaned out, and every build came out identical byte for byte, so nothing in the game moved. Four small bugs from the code review got fixed too. The nicest one: a pause of about five seconds between hits could still count as a three-hit combo and knock someone down out of nowhere. A test showed it in the old code, 3 times out of 3, and never after the fix.
