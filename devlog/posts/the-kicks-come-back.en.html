On October 11 I decided Fist II's eight attacks were too many, and we kept four. This week we built a room just to test them, and in it I found I missed the original's low and high kicks. So they're back.

{icon:chip}A room with no enemies

First I wanted a quiet place to try the moves: one room, the player alone, and no enemy ever comes in. The same moves as the level game, walking up and down the street, my own joystick in port 2. Since then it's the room where we check every change to the attacks, before it goes into the game.

We also made a move map, one page with every stick direction. Most joysticks have one button, so the rule is simple: without fire the stick walks, with fire it picks an attack.

{icon:star}What came back

The stick's four diagonals with fire are the original's again: forward and down is the low kick, back and down the reversed low kick. Forward and up is the high kick, back and up the reversed high kick. Both reversed kicks hit whoever stands behind you, without turning round.

Before, all the up diagonals did a jump kick, and I didn't like that. Now only straight up with fire is the jump kick, and it stays the blow that always knocks down.

So there are eight attacks again, but not the same eight: fire alone is a punch (in the original it did nothing), and down with fire is a punch in place of the sweep. And the enemies? They kept the original's eight attacks, just as they were.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

The reversed low kick crouches first and only then kicks, exactly as in the original. At first the automatic test thought that was a mistake, because the first move it saw was a crouch. And a test that throws every attack at real enemies found one low kick that landed from 12 pixels away. But the game itself has a rule that a blow lands only where the pictures touch, and with that rule all eight attacks touched, 2 pixels apart at the most.
