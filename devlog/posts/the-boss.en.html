Level 1 has an ending now: a boss. My graphics for him aren't drawn yet, and it didn't matter. A boss is mostly rules, so we built the rules first.

{icon:map}One line in the level file

A level's enemies come from its spawn list, written in the level's json file. One new word turns an entry into a boss: "boss": true. In level 1 he waits in the last room, at map column 640, a few steps before the exit. And until he is beaten, the exit stays shut. No "LEVEL COMPLETE" while he is standing.

{icon:fist}Who he is

Ordinary enemies come in pairs. The boss comes alone, at AI level 9, the top one a level can ask for: he blocks a lot and he answers back. He is white. The first try was purple, and on the C64's palette purple sat right next to my blue player; on screen you couldn't tell who was who.

Then the energy. A fighter's energy is one byte, so it tops out at $7f. Rather than rewrite the energy code and the HUD, the boss gets three full bars. Empty one, and he goes down, gets up, and the bar fills up again. Old arcade trick. A single jump kick or sweep won't floor him either, only a three-hit combo will.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

The same days had quieter work. I asked whether the game's source is written in my style, and we compared it with three of my own intros. Mostly yes, closest to my Blazon intro; the leftovers of the 1986 code got cleaned up, and every build came out byte-identical, so nothing in the game moved. Four small bugs from the code review got fixed too. The best one: a pause of about five seconds between blows could still count them as a three-hit combo and knock someone down out of nowhere. A test showed it on the old code, 3 times out of 3, and never after the fix.
