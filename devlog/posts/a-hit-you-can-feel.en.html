After the last playtest evening we sat down with the list of what's still missing for the fighting to feel like an arcade game, and I picked six things: four about the hit itself and two about the game around it. Six PRs, and all of them are already in the game.

{icon:star}A second to get up

Until now, anyone who fell and got up took a hit right as they were back on their feet. Two enemies could keep you on the floor round after round. Now
I want a bit more realism, so after you get up there's about a second (0.9, to be exact) when nobody can hit you. You can already move and hit back. And it works both ways: an enemy who gets up gets that second too.

{icon:fist}Flinching from the hit

In the original Fist II, a hit that doesn't knock you down only takes energy. The fighter doesn't react at all, as if nothing happened, which is a bit of a letdown... Now he flinches: he leans back for a third of a second,
gets pushed a little away from whoever hit him, and the attack he was in the middle of gets cut off. You can keep hitting him during that time, so the three-hit combo still knocks him down. The boss doesn't flinch. He's the boss.

{icon:clock}A short pause on every hit

An old arcade trick: when a hit lands, the whole fight freezes for 80 milliseconds. Four frames. The music keeps playing; only the fighters hold their pose. Too short to notice it happened, long enough to feel the hit.

{icon:map}Falling the right way

A combo that ended from behind used to throw the one who took it straight towards the one who hit them. Now, the moment someone falls, they first turn to face whoever knocked them down, then fly backwards, away from them, and get up facing them. That's how Double Dragon does it,
and now so do we. In testing: 13 knockdowns, four of them from a hit from behind, and every one of them went away from the attacker.

{icon:joystick}Three lives, and a game that plays by itself

Until now you had one life. Now you have three, with a small head icon and a number next to the score. When your energy runs out in the middle of a fight,
you don't go back to the start of the level: your energy refills, you fall, get up, and the fight carries on from the same spot. Losing the third life is GAME OVER, and CONTINUE gives you three "lives" back.

And if you leave the title screen untouched for 15 seconds, the game starts playing itself: 40 seconds of level 1 with a flashing PRESS FIRE, like an arcade machine waiting for a coin. Pressing fire takes you back to the title screen, and the HI-SCORE doesn't change.

{icon:chip}Behind the scenes

Each of the six got its own switch and a live test in VICE, which is our emulator. The fight tests also have a control build that turns the change off, to make sure the test can actually fail. And something we found along the way, while building the automatic player for the demo: the enemies block every punch and every kick from a player who stands still. Only a low kick at close range gets through, about one in five. Worth checking at the next game night.

And memory is almost full. In the gap where all the new code lives, 83 bytes are left.
